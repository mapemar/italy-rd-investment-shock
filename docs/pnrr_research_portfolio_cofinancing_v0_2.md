**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Status:** preliminary, source-based financial reconstruction plus explicit model assumptions  
**Units:** EUR million, current prices  
**Versioning:** extends the M4C2 aggregate v0.1; does not overwrite PRIN or M4C2 v0.1 files.

## 1. Updated headline estimate

The v0.1 M4C2 central estimate (EUR 1,374.5m additional R&D) is extended to include:
- M4C1 I4.1 research, PA and cultural-heritage doctoral programmes;
- the doctoral sub-line of M4C1 I3.4 (digital and green transitions);
- private co-financing associated with M4C2 extended partnerships, national centres, ecosystems, ITEC infrastructures and enterprise doctoral scholarships;
- separately identified university/host co-financing for M4C1 doctoral scholarships.

## 2. Expanded doctoral perimeter

### 2.1 M4C2 I3.3 — innovative PhDs with enterprises

The latest MUR Si.Ge.Co. v5.0 (4 September 2026) records EUR 340.4m for I3.3. ANVUR's March 2026 table, using the November 2025 CID revision, reports EUR 360m activated, EUR 340.5m committed and EUR 168.2m spent. The EUR 19.6m allocation difference is retained as a source-vintage discrepancy; this model uses the latest Si.Ge.Co. allocation and the ANVUR spending observation.

The MUR Seventh Report reports 5,956 scholarships assigned overall, 5,780 countable towards the revised target; by cycle, 1,708 (XXXVIII), 1,708 (XXXIX) and 2,489 (XL), plus 51 residual awards. The cycle counts sum to 5,905; the 51 residual awards are not assigned to a cycle in the aggregate source and are excluded from the co-financing calculation pending reconciliation.

**Call financing rules and private share:**
- **DM 352/2022 (XXXVIII cycle):** standard scholarship cost EUR 60,000; EUR 30,000 MUR/PNRR and EUR 30,000 enterprise co-financing. On 1,708 awards, the derived enterprise contribution is **EUR 51.24m**.
- **DM 117/2023 (XXXIX cycle):** EUR 30,000 MUR/PNRR plus EUR 30,000 enterprise contribution per EUR 60,000 standard scholarship. On 1,708 awards, the derived enterprise contribution is **EUR 51.24m**.
- **DM 630/2024 (XL cycle):** the MUR contribution increased to EUR 60,000 per scholarship. The enterprise is required to cover any cost above EUR 60,000; the decree does not set a uniform national top-up. On 2,489 awards, the private amount is therefore unknown. The central scenario assumes EUR 10,000 per award (EUR 24.89m), observed in an institutional 2024 call; the high scenario uses EUR 14,867 per award (EUR 37.00m) as a sensitivity benchmark, not as a national legal requirement.

Thus, the identifiable private co-financing for the first two cohorts is EUR 102.48m. The central private co-financing estimate for all three cohorts is **EUR 127.37m**, excluding the 51 residual awards until their cycle and funding rule are identified. Execution is modelled at 49.4%, equal to the ANVUR I3.3 spend/commitment ratio. This produces EUR 62.9m of estimated executed private contribution in the central case; only the Frascati-eligible and additional share enters the R&D shock.

### 2.2 M4C1 I4.1 — research, PA and cultural-heritage PhDs

ANVUR reports EUR 504m activated, EUR 499.6m committed and EUR 316.8m spent. The MUR Seventh Report records 7,237 scholarships committed, of which 7,161 countable towards the 7,200 target, across the three cycles.

The central model applies:
- R&D relevance: 75% (low 55%, high 90%);
- additionality: 50% (low 25%, high 70%).

The central additional R&D estimate from the observed EUR 316.8m public expenditure is **EUR 118.8m**. This is not the full value of the scholarships: generic research, PA and cultural-heritage doctoral activity have different R&D content, and the 75% share is a model assumption.

**University/host top-up, separate from private co-financing:** for the XXXIX cycle, the MUR contribution is EUR 60,000 per scholarship. Institutional implementation evidence reports additional university/host contributions of EUR 14,867 for a six-month foreign period and EUR 19,876 for a twelve-month period for PA and cultural-heritage scholarships. The MUR Seventh Report identifies 1,780 PA and 351 cultural-heritage awards in that cycle (2,131 total). This implies a commitment range of **EUR 31.7–42.4m**, central EUR 37.0m. It is not classified as private finance. The central model applies the I4.1 execution ratio (63.4%), 75% R&D relevance and 50% additionality, generating EUR 8.8m additional R&D. Other university top-ups under DM 351/2022 and DM 629/2024 are not quantified because no national aggregate amount was identified.

### 2.3 M4C1 I3.4 — doctoral programmes on digital and green transitions

The doctoral sub-line is EUR 34.5m, not the full EUR 272.1m I3.4 investment (which also finances Digital Education Hubs, networks of higher education schools, transnational education and AFAM internationalisation). ANVUR reports EUR 20.4m spent for the whole I3.4 investment, but does not separately report spending for the doctoral sub-line.

For a provisional execution estimate, the model allocates I3.4 spending pro rata to the doctoral sub-line: EUR 20.4m × EUR 34.5m / EUR 272.1m = **EUR 2.624m**. This is a modelled proxy, not observed doctoral expenditure. Central R&D relevance is 80% and additionality 50%, yielding EUR 1.05m additional R&D. Low/high assumptions are 60%/90% R&D relevance and 30%/70% additionality.

## 3. Reconstruction of private co-financing in M4C2

### 3.1 Extended partnerships, national centres and innovation ecosystems

For each project, the MUR award table publishes both **approved project cost** and **approved public aid**. The difference is reconstructed as the project's non-PNRR co-financing requirement:

| Measure | Projects | Approved project cost | Approved public aid | Implied co-financing gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I1.3 Extended partnerships | 14 | 1,653.698 | 1,610.000 | **43.702** |
| I1.4 National centres | 5 | 1,714.750 | 1,599.994 | **114.755** |
| I1.5 Innovation ecosystems | 11 | 1,324.617 | 1,242.801 | **81.816** |
| **Total** | **30** | **4,693.065** | **4,452.795** | **240.274** |

The MUR Si.Ge.Co. identifies private co-financing as an additional funding source for all three investments. The EUR 240.274m gap is therefore used as a provisional private co-financing proxy. However, the published aggregate award tables do not identify the legal nature of each contributing partner or split the gap by source; the proxy must be replaced by partner-level contribution records when available.

For execution, the central model applies the corresponding ANVUR measure spend/commitment ratios: 62.3% for I1.3, 60.0% for I1.4 and 66.2% for I1.5. The same measure-specific R&D-relevance and additionality assumptions used in v0.1 are then applied to the estimated executed co-financing.

### 3.2 Technological innovation infrastructures (ITEC)

The MUR call DD 3265/2021 limits the public contribution to a maximum of 49% of eligible project costs and requires private partners in a public-private partnership. The latest Si.Ge.Co. records EUR 217.057m for ITEC.

At the maximum public intensity, the minimum total eligible project cost is EUR 217.057m / 49% = EUR 443.0m, implying **minimum private co-financing of EUR 225.916m** (51% of total cost). This is a lower bound, not the observed aggregate private contribution; actual private contributions can be higher if the public contribution is below 49% of eligible cost. The model applies a 60% execution proxy, 50% central R&D relevance and 40% central additionality.

The 49% maximum is specific to ITEC. It must not be applied to research infrastructures (IR), which were financed up to 100% and are a separate sub-line.

### 3.4 From co-financing commitments to R&D performed

The co-financing envelope is not treated as spent automatically. The central estimate applies:
- execution proxies based on ANVUR's measure-level spend/commitment ratios (or a stated proxy where unavailable);
- a measure-specific Frascati R&D relevance share;
- a measure-specific additionality share.

The central EUR 593.6m private/likely-private co-financing proxy maps to:
- EUR 348.7m estimated executed contribution;
- EUR 179.9m estimated R&D performed;
- **EUR 72.1m estimated additional R&D**.

These are model outputs. The executed private contribution is not directly observed in the current evidence set.

## 4. Combined financing perimeter

The updated public allocation perimeter comprises:
- M4C2 research-oriented measures: EUR 8,284.9m (latest MUR Si.Ge.Co. v5.0);
- M4C1 I4.1 doctoral programmes: EUR 504.0m;
- M4C1 I3.4 doctoral sub-line only: EUR 34.5m.

**Combined public allocation: EUR 8,823.4m.** This excludes the remainder of M4C1 I3.4 (EUR 237.6m) because it is not a doctoral measure and excludes unrelated M4C1 education programmes.

Observed cumulative public expenditure is EUR 5,459.8m for M4C2 plus EUR 316.8m for M4C1 I4.1. The M4C1 I3.4 doctoral sub-line spend of EUR 2.624m is estimated pro rata and must remain labelled as such. The combined observed/modelled execution base is therefore approximately EUR 5,779.2m.

The central private/likely-private co-financing proxy adds EUR 593.6m to the project financing envelope; the M4C1 I4.1 university/host contribution adds a further EUR 37.0m commitment estimate. These amounts are not all actual expenditure, and only their estimated executed, Frascati-eligible and additional portions enter the R&D shock.

5. Obtain M4C1 I4.1 university/host top-ups for DM351/2022 and DM629/2024; the current EUR 31.7–42.4m range covers only the DM118/2023 PA and cultural-heritage cohort.
6. Obtain I3.4 doctoral-only expenditure; replace the pro-rata EUR 2.624m execution proxy.
7. Reconcile the I3.3 allocation vintage: EUR 340.4m in MUR Si.Ge.Co. v5.0 versus EUR 360m in ANVUR's table following the November 2025 CID revision; retain both observations and their dates.

## 9. Additional reconciliation controls

### I3.3 scholarships versus financial commitments

The MUR Seventh Report's cycle counts (1,708 + 1,708 + 2,489 = 5,905) are below the 5,956 total awards by 51 residual awards, while 5,780 are countable towards the target. The 51 residual awards are not allocated to a cycle in the aggregate evidence and remain excluded from the private co-financing calculation.

Applying the original cycle-specific public unit contributions (EUR 30k for DM352 and DM117; EUR 60k for DM630) to the three published cycle counts yields EUR 251.82m of public scholarship financing before the residual awards. ANVUR reports EUR 340.5m committed for the whole I3.3 measure. The EUR 88.7m difference is not assigned to scholarships in this model; it may reflect other I3.3 components, changes in unit contribution or further commitments, and requires a project/act-level bridge. In particular, the original I3.3 investment also envisaged incentives for firms hiring researchers. Do not automatically classify the residual as doctoral R&D.

### I4.1 award-count discrepancy

The MUR Seventh Report reports 7,237 awards committed and 7,161 countable towards the target. Its cycle breakdown (2,132 + 4,444 + 708) sums to 7,284, which is 47 above the aggregate committed count. The report also refers to a 42-scholarship residual reallocation. The award-count discrepancy is preserved rather than silently reconciled; the financial model uses the observed aggregate expenditure and the 2,131 PA/cultural-heritage cycle-XXXIX count only for the university top-up estimate.

### I3.4 doctoral spending proxy

The EUR 2.624m estimated spend is a proportional allocation of the I3.4-wide EUR 20.4m expenditure to the EUR 34.5m doctoral sub-line. It is not an observed amount and should be replaced when MUR/ReGiS reports doctoral-only expenditure.

### Co-financing classification

The EUR 240.274m gap between approved project cost and approved public aid for I1.3/I1.4/I1.5 is consistent with the MUR Si.Ge.Co. designation of private co-financing. However, the public award tables do not identify the source sector of every contribution. It is therefore retained as a **provisional private/other co-financing proxy**, not a validated business-sector funding observation. The stricter private-only floor excludes this EUR 240.274m until partner-level contributions are classified.

On the central assumptions, the combined public allocation of EUR 8,823.4m plus EUR 593.6m private/likely-private co-financing and EUR 37.0m university/host top-up implies a **total financing envelope of approximately EUR 9,454.0m**. This is a reconstructed financing envelope, not a realised expenditure total. The co-financing component is about 6.3% of the reconstructed total envelope.

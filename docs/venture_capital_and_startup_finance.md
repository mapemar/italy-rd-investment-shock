# Venture capital, start-up finance and institutional-investor reform

## Scope and analytical decision

Add the **Digital Transition Fund (DTF, M4C2 Investment 3.2)** and **Green Transition Fund (GTF, M2C2 Investment 5.4)** to the PNRR policy inventory and dashboard. Both are managed by CDP Venture Capital SGR and support start-ups/SMEs through direct and indirect financial investments. Their purpose includes strengthening the Italian innovation/start-up ecosystem and mobilising private capital.

For this project, classify them under a dedicated **venture finance / start-up ecosystem** channel. Include them in the extended innovation-finance dashboard, but do not mechanically treat their entire investment value as R&D expenditure or GERD.

## Direct and indirect transmission channels

1. **Direct investments:** DTF/GTF subscribe equity or quasi-equity instruments in eligible start-ups/SMEs; the fund documentation also describes debt/quasi-debt support at instrument level.
2. **Indirect investments:** DTF/GTF commit capital to third-party venture-capital / venture-debt funds or relevant investment vehicles. These funds then invest in portfolio companies.
3. **Private co-investment / leverage:** capture private commitments and actual co-investment separately at both fund and portfolio-company level. Do not apply a generic leverage coefficient. The DTF call for indirect investments requires private investors to commit at least an amount equal to the DTF investment in the third-party fund / relevant vehicles / target companies, creating a cumulative minimum leverage effect of 1x1. This is an indirect-investment condition, not evidence of a universal 2x multiplier. The equivalent leverage condition for direct DTF investments has not yet been verified from the direct-investment call. Therefore, record 1x1 only for the documented indirect channel and leave direct leverage unquantified.

Avoid double counting: a DTF/GTF commitment to a third-party fund is not added to the same fund's subsequent investment into a start-up as if these were two separate injections of capital. Maintain a capital-flow chain with unique transaction and fund identifiers.

## How venture capital enters the R&D analysis

Venture capital finances business formation and scaling, not necessarily R&D. A start-up may use equity for R&D, engineering, software development, regulatory compliance, sales, marketing, working capital, acquisitions or other costs. Therefore:

- Record the full amount as **venture capital mobilised / start-up finance**, not R&D.
- Identify the share used for R&D using company accounts, grant/investment documentation, audited statements, Frascati-aligned cost categories or credible company-level reporting.
- Count only R&D actually performed in Italy, irrespective of the fund manager's location.
- Assign expenditure to the year in which the R&D is performed, not the year of fund commitment, capital call or investment signing.
- Distinguish public fund capital, private co-investment, total company financing and actual company R&D expenditure.
- Estimate additionality separately: a VC investment is not automatically incremental R&D. Consider financing constraints, substitution, deadweight, displacement and the counterfactual.
- Where portfolio-company data are not available, retain the investment in the VC/start-up ecosystem dashboard but leave its R&D contribution unquantified.

This preserves the user's analytical interest in business creation and scale-up while maintaining a separate, defensible Frascati-based R&D shock.

## Institutional-investor reform: Competition Law 2024

Add **Law No. 193 of 16 December 2024**, Article 33, to the policy reform inventory as an enabling measure for institutional investment in venture-capital funds. The measure links access to the tax-favoured treatment of qualifying real-economy investments by supplementary pension funds and private pension/social-security institutions to a minimum share of qualifying investments in venture-capital funds.

The parliamentary dossier and official industrial-strategy material describe minimum thresholds of 3% for 2025, 5% for 2026 and 10% from 2027, calculated against the relevant qualifying-investment basket. The legislation was subsequently amended/clarified, including the treatment of binding commitments and indirect investment through VC funds; use the consolidated legal text and the relevant implementing interpretation before treating any threshold as a current compliance fact.

Analytical treatment:
- Classify this as an **institutional-capital mobilisation / regulatory enabling reform**, not a direct public R&D spending measure.
- Track commitments and actual capital calls separately; a binding commitment to a VC fund is not the same as cash invested or portfolio-company R&D.
- Identify additional private institutional capital mobilised, distinguishing pension funds and pension institutions from other LPs.
- Link fund commitments to underlying portfolio companies only where data allow.
- Do not convert the statutory target percentages into a forecast of VC investment or R&D without evidence on the eligible investment base, compliance, fund-raising capacity and actual deployment.

## Historical PRIN baseline

MUR's 2022 Technical Table on Fundamental Research Strategy publishes annual PRIN funding for 2007–2022 (EUR million): 110, 96, 106, 100, 75, 39, 0, 0, 92, 0, 391, 0, 0, 179, 0, 749. MUR characterises the series as irregular, with no calls in 2013–14 and 2018–19. It reports EUR 487m in 2007–11 (EUR 97.4m average) and EUR 131m in 2012–16 (EUR 26.2m average). The EUR 749m 2022 figure must be separated from the distinct EUR 420m PNRR PRIN call in November 2022 to avoid conflating national and RRF resources. The published series is annual funding/band allocation, not annual cash payments or actual R&D performance.

## Additional pre-existing national instruments

- FIT/Fondo per la crescita sostenibile: FCS replaced FIT in the 2012 incentive reform. FCS supports R&D, but also productive restructuring and internationalisation; count only R&D-specific calls and eligible costs.
- FCS regional R&D programme agreements: DM 1 April 2015, initially EUR 80m, with regional co-financing of at least 10% of public cost.
- Accordi per l'innovazione: from DM 24 May 2017, replacing the prior regional agreement regime.
- R&D tax credit: the incremental scheme under DL 145/2013 applied from 2015 and was extended to 2020; Law 160/2019 introduced the volume-based R&D/innovation/design credits from 2020. For R&D, MIMIT currently states a 20% rate through 2022 and 10% from 2023 to 2031. Separate Frascati R&D from Oslo-type technological innovation and design.

## Institutional VC reform

Law 193/2024 Article 33 creates an enabling channel by linking favourable tax treatment for qualifying real-economy investment by pension funds and social-security institutions to a minimum allocation to venture-capital funds. Current public explanatory sources report 3% in 2025, 5% in 2026 and 10% from 2027, with later amendments/interpretations clarifying binding commitments and indirect VC investments. This is not a direct R&D subsidy. Track institutional commitments, capital calls, VC deployment and portfolio-company R&D separately. Also register Law 162/2024 (Scaleup Act) as an enabling startup ecosystem reform, not as R&D expenditure.
